Deterministic-First Fact-Checking of Local News Against Multilingual Sources: A Pre-Registered Evaluation on Held-Out Articles

Alec Meeker
Bushwick Daily, Brooklyn, New York
Abstract

Background. Newsrooms that draft articles with language models need a check of every sentence against the documents the article was written from, before publication. Model-only checking is costly, varies between runs, and fails silently in ways an editor cannot audit.

System. We built a fact-check desk for a New York City local news publisher that settles what it can with deterministic code and asks a language model only about the rest. Code holds every evidence document whole, settles sentences with five rules (no checkable fact; a quotation in no document; a figure in no independent document; one passage holds the whole sentence; nothing in any document shares its content), retrieves passages for the remainder, verifies every excerpt the model cites, and re-checks every confirmed sentence for stale relative dates, date ranges, the outlets it credits and durations that contradict the article's own years. The model reads only located passages; its readings are recorded and replayed.

Evaluation. Thirteen pre-registered rounds, each on ten never-checked articles: Part A, forty factual errors planted by a blind writer agent plus ten meaning-preserving rewrites; Part B, every sentence labelled by two blind reader agents against the same evidence, with adjudication and checks outside the evidence. A round passes when at least 36 of 40 planted errors are not confirmed, at least 32 are blocked, rewrites are confirmed, no factual error is confirmed (B1), and at most 2% of the round's sentences are correct but blocked (B2).

Results. Over rounds 2-13 (2,934 sentences), the desk did not confirm 476 of 482 planted errors (98.8%) and confirmed 111 of 113 rewrites. Confirmed factual errors fell from 1-6 per round in rounds 2-7 to 0-2 in rounds 8-13; under the publisher's standard, which does not count hair-splitting or stale verb tense as factual errors, 15 of the 30 confirmed errors are withdrawn and rounds 9 and 11 pass every criterion. False blocks were 0.9%-3.9% per round (2.3% overall). A third of the articles (38 of 120) drew on documents in 15 languages other than English; on them the desk performed as on English-only articles (false blocks 2.3% and 2.3%; planted errors not confirmed 98.5% and 98.9%), and of 221 sentences it confirmed on a passage not in English, 2 were factual errors under the publisher's standard. The remaining failures are mostly the model's: a fiscal year inside a credited figure, a source's own error repeated with its credit.

Conclusion. A deterministic-first desk reaches near-complete detection of planted errors on held-out articles at about one model call per three to four sentences checked, with every decision traceable to a document. Passing every criterion in a single round is not yet reliable: rounds differ by a few sentences, within the variation of the model between runs.
