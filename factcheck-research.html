Deterministic-First Fact-Checking of AI-Drafted News: A Pre-Registered Evaluation on Unseen Articles

Alec Meeker
Bushwick Daily / Brooklyn New Media, New York
Abstract

A newsroom that drafts news with language models has to check every sentence against the sources it holds. This paper describes a deterministic-first fact-checking desk and its evaluation. Code reads every sentence against every held document and settles what it can: a quotation or figure that no source holds, a passage that states the whole sentence. A language model is asked only about the remaining sentences, is shown the passages code retrieved, and code verifies every excerpt the model cites against the whole document it names. Sources in other languages are read by meaning with a multilingual embedding model that runs on the newsroom's own machine.

The desk was evaluated in pre-registered test rounds on articles it had never checked. In each round a blind writer planted 40 single-fact errors into real sentences, and two blind readers labelled every sentence against the held evidence. Across six rounds (1,422 sentences), the desk did not confirm 235 of 240 planted errors (97.9%) and blocked 233 (97.1%); blocks on correct sentences fell from 3.7% in the first round to between 0.9% and 2.9% in rounds three to six. On the articles whose sources were in Spanish and Italian, reading them by meaning cut blocks on correct sentences from 8 to 0. The strictest criterion, no confirmed factual error in a round, has not yet been met: rounds two to six confirmed 1, 5, 3, 3 and 6 errors among 129 to 290 confirmed sentences. Each failure was diagnosed, fixed, measured offline, and tested again on new articles in the next round.

Keywords fact-checking, digital news, LLM evaluation, pre-registration, cross-language verification, retrieval

1The desk

Every article is checked sentence by sentence (headline, body, SEO title and description) against an evidence snapshot: every document the newsroom's routes delivered for the article, every document an editor attached, and every page the article links, each read whole. Code settles a sentence when it can decide it without judgment: no checkable fact; a quotation no held document contains; a figure no independent source holds; one passage that states the whole sentence, with its names, dates and figures bound to the same source sentence. A language model (Claude) judges only the rest. Each answer is stored and replayed on later runs instead of being asked again, and a sentence the desk cannot support blocks publication until an editor fixes it.

2Sources in other languages

In the newsroom's articles, 66 of 208 hold a source document that is not in English. In the first test round, 8 of the 9 blocks on correct sentences were in the two articles whose sources were Spanish and Italian: the desk read evidence with tools that match English words, so a quotation the writer had translated looked fabricated, and keyword retrieval never showed the original passage. Measured before shipping, keyword retrieval showed a supporting excerpt for 22 of 31 held-out sentences with non-English support; one additional passage retrieved by meaning (intfloat/multilingual-e5-small, run on the newsroom's own machine) brought it to 30 of 31. With the fix, the model is shown passages by meaning, judges an English quotation against the original-language words, and a quotation held only in another language is no longer blocked by code. On those two articles, blocks on correct sentences fell from 8 to 0 and model calls from 11 to 6. Round six included an article whose sources are all in Urdu; it exposed one more code rule that matched only English names and figures, which blocked five correct sentences; that rule now defers to the model when a source is not in English, and when the article was checked again none of the five was blocked.

3Evaluation method

Each round's protocol was committed to version control after ten articles were drawn at random from the never-checked queue and before the desk ran on any of them. Part A: a blind writer agent, which never saw the desk's code or results, changed exactly one fact in 40 confirmed sentences (numbers, dates, people, places, outcomes, attributions) and reworded 10 more with every fact kept. Part B: two blind readers labelled every sentence of the round against the held evidence, and an adjudicator decided every disagreement, recording outside checks with raw copies. The pre-registered criteria: A1, at least 36 of 40 planted errors not confirmed; A2, at least 32 blocked; A3, the rewrites confirmed; B1, no desk-confirmed sentence that is a factual error; B2, blocks on correct sentences at most 2% of the round.

5In production

The desk has checked every machine-drafted article since June 2026. By September 2026 it had run 5,165 checks over 2,729 drafts, verifying 96,765 claims and 13,735 quotes and blocking 1,014 drafts for correction (the production report).

6Limitations

Each round has ten articles and forty planted errors, so the intervals are wide. The writer, the readers and the desk's model are models of the same family, and the adjudicator built the fixes; every adjudication decision is recorded with its evidence. The complete record (protocols, deviations, every model call with its prompt and tokens, the code at each version, and the readers' labels) is held in a peer-review package; it includes private newsroom evidence and is available to reviewers on request.
